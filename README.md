# First PR Practice

This is a small practice repository for learning the GitHub pull request workflow:
create a branch, make a change, open a PR, and merge it.

## What this repo does

Nothing yet — it's a sandbox for practicing git and GitHub.

## Getting started

1. Clone the repo.
2. Make a change on a new branch.
3. Open a pull request and mege it.

## ScholarPath

**Full project specification: [`docs/SCHOLARPATH_SPEC.md`](docs/SCHOLARPATH_SPEC.md)** — requirements, rules, design, architecture, limitations and change log. Notes for Claude sessions: [`CLAUDE.md`](CLAUDE.md). Live page: https://claude.ai/artifact/RQWX4dnGb7W68zTghnoLvz

`index.html` is **ScholarPath**, a single-page research companion for master's students and early researchers.
Open the file in any modern browser. It needs no install, build step or login, and all work is saved in the browser's localStorage.

### Run it on your own computer (localhost)

1. Download the project as a ZIP (on GitHub: **Code → Download ZIP**, or [this branch's ZIP](https://github.com/dimacodes01/first-pr-practice/archive/refs/heads/claude/scholarpath-research-app-jabga7.zip)) and unzip it.
2. Double-click the starter for your computer:
   - **Windows:** `Start-ScholarPath-Windows.bat`
   - **Mac:** `Start-ScholarPath-Mac.command` (the first time, right-click it and choose **Open**, because macOS blocks downloaded scripts)
3. Your browser opens **http://localhost:8000/index.html**. Keep the small black window open while you use ScholarPath, and close it when you finish.

The localhost address only works while that window is open. The starters need Python; without it they open `index.html` straight from the folder instead, which works the same. Claude features (simple versions, translations, Ask Claude, figure explanations) need the published claude.ai page; everything else works locally. Work saved on localhost is separate from the claude.ai page: use **My Progress → Back up** and restore to move it.

The app is organised as a journey in four parts: **1 · Write a paper** (writing path, find literature, ideas & reading list, figures & tables), **2 · Critique a paper** (read the paper, explain the figures, write the AAU critique, trace where each statement comes from), **3 · Present** (build the slides, prepare for questions) and **4 · Verify & defend** (the final academic verification and consistency checklist, then the professor questions and a mock professor), plus a Dashboard (*Your Research Workspace*: a progress summary, this week's calendar of the steps you opened, continue researching, your library and the research tools) and My Progress.

The look (since 13 Oct 2026) is a friendly school-platform design, like the owner's own school learning platform: a dark-blue top bar, a slate sidebar with a white tile for the current page, white cards on a light blue-grey page, rounded pill buttons, underlined blue tabs and colourful icons and initials. It uses Nunito, with IBM Plex Sans Arabic for Arabic, Lucide icons and no emoji, in light and dark mode, and every text passes WCAG AA contrast. A collapsible sidebar lists every step (a drawer on tablets and phones), and the header shows where you are, with a **Full screen** button where the browser allows it.

| Tab | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Resource Hub | Cards for 10 academic databases, a search-link builder (keywords + optional start year), and search tips |
| Idea Organizer | Idea cards (title, research question, keywords, tags, status), status and tag filters, and a reading list linked to ideas |
| Paper Reader | Three reading modes: **Read** (the paper on its own, with a small toolbar under the selected paragraph and the study panel as a drawer), **Study** (outline · paper · study panel) and **Compare** (the original beside its Simple English or Arabic version, kept in step paragraph by paragraph). Separately, the version shown and read aloud: Original, Simple English or Arabic. The paper reads as one continuous document (Source Serif 4, ¶ numbers in the margin, the title page folded away); the study panel has Explain, Arabic (translation, an Arabic explanation and English–Arabic key terms), Notes (main idea, my notes, highlighted important concepts), Ask Claude and Paper (the exact source of the paragraph and the whole-paper tools). A full-width audio bar along the bottom never covers the text, and switching mode never loses your place. Upload a PDF (or paste text): it is split into paragraphs by section and page. Figures and tables (with their captions and the text inside them) are kept out of the reading: a picture of each appears where it sits in the paper, with a button that opens it in Figure Lab for explanation and critique. For each paragraph: the main idea in simple words, key terms, your own memory hook and a link to an earlier idea. Each paragraph shows the full original text, then a simple version (the same paragraph rewritten in clear English), then its main idea; both — plus an optional Arabic translation (by Claude, or typed in) — can be read aloud with the most natural voices on your device: American, British, Australian or Canadian English (when the device has them), Modern Standard Arabic by default with Gulf, Levantine or Egyptian speakers where available, voice previews, no pitch faking; academic text is spoken properly (statistics, abbreviations, citations, acronyms, a personal pronunciation list), English terms inside Arabic are read by the English voice, and Claude can add Arabic vowel marks for listening; read a selection, a paragraph or a section; replay, next and previous sentence; speed and volume; pause anywhere and Continue from the same word (remembered across reloads); and an *Ask Claude* chat answers questions about a paragraph or the whole paper. Also an *Idea flow* view, and presentation questions (you ask the class / the professor may ask you / colleagues may ask you). On the published claude.ai page, Claude writes the summaries and paper-specific questions using the viewer's own account; elsewhere you write your own, with starter questions. |
| Paper Critique Studio | The official Al Ain University Advanced Research Methods (0106112) Paper Critique Form (paper information, Part A diagnostic ratings, Part B sections 1–8 with weights, word limits and live word counts, 10 related references with verification flags, reserved instructor section, and a 12-point quality-control check). Also: in-browser PDF text search with page numbers, a copyable AI-assistant prompt, copyable text and Word (.docx) export. A general 11-section template is also available. |
| Write Your Paper | 10-step writing path with explanations, checklists and notes, plus an IMRaD template, an IEEE structure guide, a generated outline and a self-review checklist |
| Figure Lab | **Analyse a paper's figures:** upload a PDF; every figure and table is found by its caption, cropped from the page and linked to the sentences that cite it, and (on the published page) Claude explains and critiques each one — strengths, weaknesses, importance to the study, effect on its conclusions and relation to the research question. **Explain a figure:** figure details linked to a critique, 10 figure types with how-to-read steps and red flags, guided questions, a 10-item quality checklist, 1–5 ratings, and a generated figure explanation that can be added to the critique's Results section. **Build a figure:** goal-based figure recommendations with previews, a live chart builder (bar, grouped bar, line, scatter) with SVG, table and IEEE-sized matplotlib code, an IEEE caption and alt-text builder, and figure-writing and IEEE rules panels. |
| Presentation Builder | Add your critique (Word, PDF, text or paste), outline the slides from it (with Claude, or from its own sections), edit them, and generate the whole PowerPoint (.pptx) with speaker notes and timings for 5/10/15-minute talks |
| Evidence map | **Where Did This Come From? — Source Evidence Mapping.** Every statement of your critique (or slides) linked to the exact passage of the paper: page, section, paragraph and the original words, side by side with matching highlights, and on the real PDF page when it is stored. Each statement is classified (direct, paraphrased, critical interpretation, partially supported, unsupported, external) by Claude, but a quotation is used only if ScholarPath finds it word for word in the paper, and pages and headings always come from the paper — otherwise "Source not verified". Explain the connection, Show my evidence for selected words, a filterable mapping table with CSV/Word export, and links from the reader, the critique, verification and professor questions. |
| Verify | **Final Academic Verification & Consistency Checklist.** Compares the original paper (the source of truth), the written critique and the presentation — each from ScholarPath or uploaded (PDF, Word `.docx`, PowerPoint `.pptx`) or pasted. A 51-item checklist in six sections, a 13-row consistency matrix, contradiction detection (exact quotes, locations, evidence, correction), corrections that keep factual and editorial changes apart (including corrected Word/PowerPoint copies that keep the design), automatic number/wording/citation checks, and a QA report (A–G) with Word export. Every result remembers the document versions it was made on and asks for a re-check when one changes; nothing counts as checked until it has been checked. Claude runs the checks on the published page (or copy the prompt and paste the reply back). |
| Professor questions | **Potential Professor Questions & Critical Discussion Preparation.** Uses the same three documents. Claude first analyses the paper, the critique's own headings and the slides, then writes 5–8 questions per critique section, challenges to your strengths, weaknesses and recommendations, and 2–3 questions per slide — each with what the professor means, a detailed and a short oral answer, evidence, a follow-up and a priority. No fixed question bank: the questions change with the paper and are marked for regenerating when a document changes. A Q&A checklist, a mock professor (one question at a time, feedback on your answer, follow-ups, voice input where allowed) and a report (A–F) with Word export. A question counts as prepared only after you have answered it. |
| My Progress | Dashboard of counts and progress (including figures reviewed), plus backup/restore (JSON), remove examples and reset |

The app opens with one example idea, two example papers and an example AAU-form critique of *Gender Shades* (2018), all clearly labelled. You can remove them from **My Progress**.

`vendor/pdfjs/` holds pdf.js 3.11.174 (Apache-2.0, see its LICENSE) so PDFs can be read without a CDN (figure crops are kept in the browser's IndexedDB); the app falls back to cdnjs if the folder is missing. `vendor/pptxgenjs/` holds PptxGenJS 4.0.1 (MIT, see its LICENSE), which makes the PowerPoint in step 3.1 in the browser; it falls back to jsDelivr.
